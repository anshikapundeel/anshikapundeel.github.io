Personal engineering case study

What happens when ClickHouse stops accepting writes?

Two 18-second trials used the same 3,000-sample queue, two workers and an eight-retry limit. Blocking slowed the producer. Dropping new samples kept it running through the outage.

Block policy

Block delivered 18,238 of 22,640 offered samples (80.6%). DropNewest delivered 25,960 of 36,000 (72.1%). Both resumed delivery. The policy with the lower delivery fraction still delivered more samples because the producer kept offering work. For a source that can pause, the loss fraction matters. For a source that must keep sampling, count what it never got to offer as well.

These single-host trials show why the denominator matters. To compare policies at identical demand, the next experiment would replay the same timestamped input into both. Read the experiment and reproduction steps.

TelemetryScale

Go telemetry ingestion with bounded queues, batching and retry. In a 10-second single-host ClickHouse trial, a 200 ms flush interval delivered 14,955 samples/s. During an induced outage, blocking slowed producers while dropping new samples preserved the offered rate at the cost of more lost samples.
